The eight stages

STAGE 1

Foundations: how AI really works

Lesson ready

Why for you: your biggest gaps were underneath: what gets sent each message, how tools and MCP layer, quantization, git and JSON. Every later stage stands on these.

You'll learn
  • One request, start to finish: the package, overflow, caching, chat templates
  • The four layers, and how tools, MCP, skills and plugins plug in
  • How agents see their environment, and API vs. browser vs. mouse
  • Builder basics, memory and RAG, how models are made, your RX 580
Already yours (skim)
  • Tokens, temperature, system prompts
  • Agents as loops, subagents
  • APIs, ports, keys, hosting
Build: an environment briefing for your Odysseus model, and your own tokens-per-second numbers for a 9B model and a 30B-A3B model.
STAGE 2

Measure, don't guess: evals like a pro

Next

Why for you: you already invented the core of this in Yo: run it 10 times, compare to a baseline, pick A or B. This stage turns that instinct into a real method, and it comes before design because you can't improve what you can't measure.

You'll learn
  • Baselines and luck: how many runs it takes before a difference is real
  • Three kinds of grader: hidden tests, AI judges, and pairwise A-vs-B
  • Keeping the exam honest: hidden answers, checking the grader catches wrong work
  • Tracing: recording every package and tool call so you can see why something failed
  • Cost and speed as scores, not just "did it work"
  • Tools: promptfoo (its repeat setting) and Langfuse for traces
Already yours
  • Run it many times, never trust the first response
  • Compare against the old version
  • Let the model check its own work
Build: finish Paperclip's 6-task exam, measure the luck gap, then compare two setups (for example, medium vs. low thinking).
STAGE 3

Designing agent systems

Later

Why for you: Paperclip has a CEO, a Reviewer and hand-offs. This stage teaches when extra agents help, when they just burn tokens, and where a fast layer like Jev fits.

You'll learn
  • Context engineering as the core job of a harness
  • Orchestrator and workers: how Anthropic's own research system is built, and its costs (agents use about 4x the tokens of chat, multi-agent about 15x)
  • Routing: cheap, fast decision models (like Jev) for small choices, big models for hard thinking
  • Tool design, memory files (CLAUDE.md, an Obsidian vault of notes), hooks and permissions
  • Common failures: endless loops, vague hand-offs, agents grading their own homework
Already yours
  • Fewer tools is better
  • Remove risky tools rather than trust prompts
Build: use your Stage 2 exam to test whether Paperclip's Reviewer actually raises the score, and keep it only if it does.
STAGE 4

Research agents you can trust

Later

Why for you: you asked why AI keeps lacking current information, and how to get an agent that finds transcripts and repos the way you do.

You'll learn
  • Search vs. actually opening the page, and why the difference matters
  • The core rule: cite only pages it opened, with a quote and a URL
  • Why AI citations go wrong so often, and how to catch it
  • The tools: last30days, yt-dlp for transcripts (and its limits), GitHub MCP in read-only mode
  • Saving research as dated markdown notes (Obsidian), not hype
Already yours
  • Compare against other sources
  • Search tools add fresh facts
Build: a research agent that writes a cited brief, graded on 10 of your own questions with the Stage 2 method.
STAGE 5

Reverse engineering with agents

Later

Why for you: REA and Universal Modder are your core tools, and you want to understand how mechanics are built before improving on them.

You'll learn
  • Source code vs. compiled programs, and what a decompiler gets back
  • Unity specifics: Mono builds keep .NET code that decompiles well, IL2CPP builds are much harder
  • Static analysis (reading) vs. dynamic analysis (running), and why running needs a VM
  • The workflow: understand a mechanic, write a spec, re-implement it, test it
  • REA with Ghidra or JEB, Universal Modder's skills, and the legal lines
Already yours
  • Understand first, then improve, not copy
Build: run REA on your own Unity build, where you already have the source, and grade its explanations against the real code. You know the right answers, which makes it the perfect test of REA itself.
STAGE 6

Image and video pipelines

Later

Why for you: your content pipeline, and your question about how well image generators understand context, which is their weak spot.

You'll learn

  • Hosted services (Higgsfield, fal) and credit costs
  • Automating script, assets, edit and upload
  • Platform rules: copyright claims, "reused content" policies and strikes, so you know the risks of reposting
Already yours
  • Pick A or B and record why
Build: the free ambient-video pipeline first, with your A-or-B picks flowing into the learning agent.

Already yours

  • Embeddings as a space of meanings
